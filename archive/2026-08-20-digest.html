01 Denver-Area Updates

Launch Family Entertainment signs lease for its first Colorado location

Denver MetroConstructionReported June 4, 2026

Launch Family Entertainment will open its first Colorado hub in the Yosemite Park Shopping Center at 9090 E. Phillips Place, Centennial. The 35,350-sq-ft venue will combine bowling, arcade games, mini golf, trampolines, a mixed-reality playground, and a toddler soft-play area, plus the brand's KRAVE restaurant and bar. Construction is slated to begin in August 2026, with the location targeting an early-2027 opening.

Sky Zone's sixth Colorado park advances toward Highlands Ranch

Denver MetroConstructionFranchise in development

Local franchisee couple Nick and Lisa Nettleton continue to move the sixth Colorado Sky Zone toward opening in Highlands Ranch. A precise address and opening date have not yet been announced; recent Denver-market roundups still list it as the next indoor-trampoline addition to watch in the south-metro suburbs.

Source: skyzone.com · 9News

Elitch Gardens opens its 136th season with an expanded kids' lineup

DenverProgrammingSeason opened April 18, 2026

Denver's Elitch Gardens opened for 2026 with 54 rides, slides, and attractions, including 14 kid-sized rides in KiddieLand and full access to the Island Kingdom water park on select days. New festival weekends, drone shows, and dive-in movies round out the season. Kroenke Sports & Entertainment, now sole owner of the surrounding River Mile site, has reaffirmed the park will keep operating at its current downtown location.

LEGO Group completes $268M buyout of 29 Discovery Centres from Merlin

NationalIndustry consolidationCompleted Feb 27, 2026

The LEGO Group closed its acquisition of LEGO and LEGOLAND Discovery Centres from Merlin Entertainments for roughly £0.2B ($268M), bringing 29 sites across nine countries — about five million annual visitors and 1,500 employees — under direct LEGO Group control. Merlin keeps operating the larger LEGOLAND destination resorts under long-term license; this deal covers only the smaller mall-based Discovery Centres, several of which are US properties (New Jersey, Michigan, Philadelphia, and others).

04 Marketing Trends

Nostalgia-driven and "grown-up" spinoffs are the season's marketing play

TrendNational

Chuck E. Cheese's "Chuck's Arcade" concept — targeting millennial parents' own nostalgia rather than only their kids — and Great Wolf Lodge's season-pass push both point to operators broadening beyond single-visit family trips toward repeat, cross-generational engagement and loyalty products.

AR/VR and mixed-reality attractions are now a standard build-out line item

TrendNational

Location-based VR entertainment is projected to grow at a 7.8% CAGR from 2026–2031, and new builds — including Launch Family Entertainment's Centennial, CO location — are opening with mixed-reality playgrounds as a headline attraction rather than an add-on, alongside deeper food-and-beverage integration to extend dwell time.

Source: Mordor Intelligence, Family Entertainment Center Market Report

Social-media reaction is now part of the opening-week playbook — for better or worse

TrendNational

Universal Kids Resort's launch showed both sides: it dominated family-travel content while also drawing viral criticism over pricing and crowds. Meow Wolf's highly photographable rooms remain a case study in the opposite pattern — organic, largely positive social spread driving ticket sales without a traditional ad push.

Industry outlook: consolidation and category growth continue in parallel

Market dataNational

The global family entertainment center market is estimated at $34.57B in 2026, on pace for $47.93B by 2031 (6.75% CAGR), even as the LEGO Group's Merlin acquisition signals brand owners taking direct control of experiential retail rather than licensing it out. IAAPA's 2026 FEC Summit programming is centered on exactly this tension — growth opportunity alongside operational and ownership consolidation.
