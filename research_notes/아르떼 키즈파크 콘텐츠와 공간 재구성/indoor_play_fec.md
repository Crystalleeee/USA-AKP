# Indoor Play Centers, FECs and Asian Kids-Cafe / Media Playground Comparables for Arte Kids Park USA

Research date: 2026-11-05. Covered in about 17 tool calls, so coverage is broad but shallow. All pricing comes from location pages or third-party guides and varies by site. Items marked UNVERIFIED have no source I could confirm.

## Q1. US comparables: concept, content mix, zoning, age targeting, tickets and memberships

### Takeaway
The US market falls into three groups:
1. Large active-entertainment franchises built on trampolines and adventure attractions for ages 3–17: Sky Zone, Urban Air, Altitude.
2. Mid-size climbing and soft-play adventure parks: Funtopia, Kids Empire.
3. Small play cafés and sensory or therapy gyms for ages 0–6: play cafés, Little Land, We Rock the Spectrum.

The big franchises earn recurring revenue mainly from monthly memberships. Sky Zone has about 800k members. Play cafés sell 2-hour sessions for $12–20 and monthly memberships for $35–80. No major US chain is built mainly around interactive projection. Interactive floor and wall systems such as Valo show up as add-on attractions inside trampoline parks and FECs.

### Inferences
- Sky Zone's 15% member growth and its attraction count rising from about 4 to 40+ suggest that US repeat visits come from memberships plus a steady addition of new attractions. Arte Kids Park USA would need a comparable refresh pipeline. With media content, a refresh can be a software update rather than a hardware install.
- Sky Zone's move into a ≤6 toddler concept (Cloudbound) shows that large operators see room in the 0–6 segment. AKP's plan to include infants with parents fits that trend.
- Valo's figures (110 players/hour, short game rounds) give a benchmark for throughput of 1–3 minute interactive content in a US FEC.
- US price anchors: play cafés charge about $15–20 for 2 hours. Trampoline park memberships run about $20–50/month, often capped at one visit per day or 1–2 hours.

### Gaps
- I found no public FDD Item 19 figures for Urban Air, Sky Zone, Altitude, Kids Empire or WRTS in this pass. They would need state franchise registries such as the CA DFPI or WI DFI.

- I found no 2026 Urban Air membership pricing from an official source.
- I found no US interactive-media-first kids playground chain. A dedicated search is needed to confirm none exists.

## Q2. Asia / Middle East comparables, including Korean kids-cafe trends and d'strict's own Arte Kids Park Korea

### Takeaway
d'strict already runs Arte Kids Park in Korea at two sites:
- Jeju Aewol: about 1,400 pyeong, opened April 2024.
- Namyangju Byeollae: about 600 pyeong, opened July 10, 2026.

The Korean format sells a 3-hour child ticket (Jeju: KRW 33,000) and a separate guardian ticket. It mixes physical play with media walls, a live sketchbook, a sand beach and motion games. Byeollae also uses a tagging band for mission and point collection. The US plan explicitly drops that mechanic.

In Asia, mall-based all-you-can-play chains are the norm. Kidzooona (AEON Fantasy) runs 200+ outlets with infant zones and role-play towns. Character parks such as Pororo Park run 2–3 hour sessions. Korea's kids-cafe market is moving toward premium department-store venues and a fast-growing public "Seoul-type kids café" network.

### Inferences
- The Korean Arte Kids Park formats are the closest comparables. The core content blocks carry over to the US plan: media wall + Live Sketchbook (creation), Sand Beach (exploration), motion floor/bike games (repetitive play), and a guardian lounge facing media art. Byeollae's 600 pyeong (about 21,000 sq ft) is a realistic US mall-box size. For reference, Funtopia Naperville is 25k sq ft and the Sky Zone AUV is $2.2M.
- Byeollae's tag-band mission system is the main thing the US version drops. Without collection mechanics, repeat visits in the US must come from content refresh, memberships, classes (Live Atelier) and parties.
- The Asian session norm (2–3 hours, guardian ticket sold separately) differs from US play cafés, which charge per child for 2 hours with parents usually free, and from trampoline parks, which sell 60–120 minute jump times. Charging for guardians would be unusual in the US.

Middle East chains such as Magic Planet and Fun City were not covered.

## Q3. Content refresh, seasonal programming, memberships and industry operating metrics (IAAPA / FEC data)

### Takeaway
Published FEC benchmarks are old (IAAPA FY2016: 2.7-hour visit, 2.8 visits per year) or come from vendors. Figures for birthday parties' share of revenue range from 14% to 40%+, depending on the source. The most-cited figure is 15–20%, an IAAPA rule of thumb. Operators drive repeats mainly through recurring monthly memberships and new attractions added over time. Play cafés also rely on multi-visit punch passes.

### Cited Findings
- IAAPA FEC Benchmark Report (FY2016 data): average visit 2.7 hours, guests visit 2.8 times per year, birthday parties remain the most lucrative special event. — [Trainertainment summary of IAAPA Benchmark](https://trainertainment.net/iaapa-benchmark-despite-diversity-there-is-much-to-be-learned-from-fellow-fecs/)
- Birthday parties are commonly cited at 15–20% of FEC revenue, a figure attributed to IAAPA Expo sessions (2017). — [Perfect Game Marketing](https://perfectgamemarketing.com/book-more-birthday-parties-fec/)
- A 2025 "family entertainment revenue mix" puts admissions at 40%, attractions/rides 19%, F&B 16% and birthday parties 14%. The original data source is unclear. — [market.us](https://market.us/report/children-entertainment-centers-market/)
- Vendor claims say birthday + F&B can reach 30–40% of turnover in optimized venues. — [Global Fun Sports](https://globalfunsports.com/news/investments-and-business-plan/how-to-design-a-birthday-and-fb-zone-to-generate-up-to-40-of-your-amusement-parks-revenue); [Valo Motion blog](https://www.valomotion.com/blog/birthday-party-revenue)
- One FEC design guide says success depends on 6+ repeat visits per year. — [Adventure Solutions](https://www.adventuresolutionsus.com/post/what-is-a-family-entertainment-center/)
- Sky Zone's food and drink share is about 5% of sales. That is far below the 16% F&B in the generic mix above, which suggests trampoline-park economics depend on admission and membership. — [Franchise Times](https://www.franchisetimes.com/top-400-2025/exclusive-research/new-attractions-help-sky-zone-push-major-systemwide-sales-increase/article_89546094-0619-498f-820a-2dfbaadd9ee2.html)
- Content refresh examples:
  - Sky Zone grew from about 4 to 40+ attractions and credits new attractions for its 2024 sales growth. — [Franchise Times](https://www.franchisetimes.com/top-400-2025/exclusive-research/new-attractions-help-sky-zone-push-major-systemwide-sales-increase/article_89546094-0619-498f-820a-2dfbaadd9ee2.html)
  - AREA 254 added more Valo units after about a year and re-bundled them into a pass. — [Valo case study](https://www.valomotion.com/case-study/area254-killeen)
- Membership structures seen:
  - Monthly auto-renew, one visit per day, time-of-day limits (Sky Zone). — [Sky Zone](https://www.skyzone.com/san-diego-miramar-ca/memberships/)
  - Single-location vs. tiered passes (Kids Empire). — [Kids Empire](https://www.kidsempire.com/memberships)
  - Sibling add-on pricing (Bright Bean, +$20). — [ParentMap](https://www.parentmap.com/things-to-do/indoor-play-cafe-seattle-tacoma-bellevue/)
  - Non-expiring 10-visit packs (Lattes and Littles). — [Lattes and Littles](https://lattesandlittles.com/openplay)
- I found no reliable benchmark for converting party guests into return visitors. A retention vendor claims a 15–20% baseline, rising to 35–45% with follow-up, with no source given. UNVERIFIED.

### Inferences
- For a 1–3 minute session format, a 2.7-hour average FEC visit would mean about 30–60 content plays per visit. A broad content library and frequent rotation (seasonal skins and themes on media walls) are the media-native way to match Sky Zone's attraction-addition model at lower capex.
- If parties are 15–20% of revenue, the planned party rooms are material. The Live Atelier could double as a party add-on and as a class product sold as a membership perk.
- A US membership design for AKP could take a monthly per-child base (about $30–50 benchmark), add a sibling add-on, and use weekday or time-window limits to manage peak load.

### Gaps
- I found no current (2023–2026) IAAPA FEC benchmark with dwell time, visit frequency or party share. The report is likely behind IAAPA's member paywall.
- I found no published dwell time or repeat-visit rates for Sky Zone, Urban Air, play cafés, Kidzooona or Arte Kids Park.
- I found no documented seasonal programming calendars for specific brands, such as Kidzooona events or Sky Zone Glow nights. These are commonly marketed, but I did not source them in this pass.
